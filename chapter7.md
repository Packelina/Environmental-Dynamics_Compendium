# Chapter 7: Anthropogenic metabolism Human-induced material flows in the Technosphere

Anthropogenic metabolism is a term used to describe flows of materials or substances within and across the boundaries of the anthroposphere (or technosphere). Everywhere human activities take place (referred to as anthroposphere or as in the compendium as technosphere) man-made material flows (driven by man) interfere with natural material flows (driven by nature) which may be quantified using different flow analysis tools. The high and steadily increasing material flows of and reservoirs in our wealthy society cause problems in terms of natural resource depletion and environmental pollution. Sustainable development therefore requires analysis of material flows in the technosphere.

Material flow analysis methods provide information about relevant flows and reservoirs of a particular material within a specific system. Through balancing inputs and outputs, the flows of wastes and environmental loadings become visible, and their sources can be identified. Further, the depletion or accumulation of materials within the system can be identified. The following chapter focuses on modeling material flows such as nitrogen in the anthropogenic systems. The presented methods, however, can also be used for other materials/substances like for example related to energy consumption and information flows.
