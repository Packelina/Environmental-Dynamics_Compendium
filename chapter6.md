# Chapter 6: Climate change: Linking atmosphere, biosphere, hydrosphere and technosphere

**Is something happening with our climate? Are we experiencing more intense rain events that come more frequently? Have floods and storms become more severe? Are heat waves getting more extreme, and this even in Europe? What about winters starting later and ending earlier? Are glaciers melting around the world and is the global mean sea level rising? Is this all due to "climate change" or can it just be attributed to natural climate variability?**

The impact of mankind on climate change has been debated for several decades. Are we living in a world with human-affected climate causing climate and weather extremes or are we just experiencing natural climate/ weather variations? The term "climate change" is commonly used to refer to changes in the earth's global climate or regional climates. It describes changes in the variability or average state of the atmosphere - or average weather - over any time scale from decades to millions of years. These changes can be caused by internal processes, external forces or, most recently, by human activities.

The fact is that greenhouse gases are released into the atmosphere due to human activities and rising levels of such gases may change the climate. The global temperature may increase with about 1.4 - 5.8°C by the year 2100 if we believe certain climate model predictions ({ref}`fig_temperature_prediction_model`). On the one hand, this would imply significant, direct impacts on the global environment, including increasing precipitation, flooding and frequency of extreme events mentioned above. On the other hand, this may then also put new or more pressure on human society in terms of food security and water supply.
