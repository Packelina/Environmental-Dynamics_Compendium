# 4.1. Introduction - Sins past and present: Can subsurface quality be revived

**Why is groundwater important? Why do you need to learn about groundwater? Will you ever run into situations in your career where this knowledge is relevant? We will discuss these questions briefly in the following section.**

Living in a society where clean water is readily available from the tap or turning on the shower, it is common to take clean water for granted. However, looking at a global scale, it is a completely different situation. One third of the Earth's population are not blessed with these kind of luxuries. They have to live with water supplies below the UN recommendations for minimum need of fresh water and for many millions the water situation is a matter of life and death.

The vast growing population in many countries is putting high pressure on existing water resources. An article from @bbc2004 states that **"Global water consumption rose sixfold between 1990 and 1995 - more than double the rate of population growth"** and continues **"consumption will soar as more people expect Western-style lifestyles and diets"**. Managing such increasing water demands requires sustainable use of the available water resources. This means for example to prevent mining and pollution of already existing water resources.

The fact that 95% of the freshwater on earth is in the form of groundwater, gives a hint on the importance of this water resource. In Sweden more than 75% of the population is to some extent depending on groundwater withdrawn from glaciofluvial sand and gravel deposits forming a high yielding aquifer covering a limited area of the country (@lenntech2006). In the United States around 53% of the population relies on groundwater for domestic purposes, and looking at many developing countries we see an even higher dependence.

With this in mind, the issue of water quantity, i.e. providing water for growing demands, is obvious. However, another important issue is to maintain the quality of used water resources. That means to prevent the resources from being polluted by water using and impacting sectors. Important questions in this context include: What is groundwater contamination? What are the sources of contamination? How do we assess groundwater contamination? What are the various options in order to clean up and prevent groundwater contamination?

However, knowledge about groundwater is not only important when considering quantitative and qualitative aspects of water-resources. The list below shows a few other examples:

- Groundwater has to be considered in geotechnical engineering. For example when assessing the stability of the ground, since a change in the groundwater conditions might result in reduced stability and risk for settling of the ground.
- For a civil engineer the groundwater issue arises for example when constructing a house with a foundation below the water table. Questions like the following are relevant: How much water needs to be pumped to keep the water level below the excavation level? Where should the pumps be located? Will it influence groundwater installations in the vicinity of the construction site?
- Another example is the construction of dams. If groundwater issues are neglected, this could result in water seeping through the ground below the dam, rendering the construction useless.
- In tunneling construction, subsurface water has to be kept out. A recent example where the complexity of this issue was underestimated, was the initial construction of the tunnel through Hallandsås in southern Sweden. In this project the neglect resulted in large extra expenses, breaking deadlines and it also had a negative impact on the environment.
- You might run into a situation where the company you are working for is handling or is involved in processes that result in the release of pollutants to the groundwater. Storing and handling hazardous substances, for example radioactive waste, involves dealing with the possible spread of contaminants through the subsurface.

Many of the issues discussed in this section involve the prediction on how the groundwater behaves and/or how it will react to some external influence. The study encompassing these issues is commonly termed hydrogeology. This is a very broad term and spans many different fields like: geology, hydrology, mathematics, statistics, chemistry etc.
