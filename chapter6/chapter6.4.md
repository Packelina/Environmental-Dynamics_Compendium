# 6.4. Concluding thoughts

Earth's climate has always changed over time with periods of glaciation followed by warm periods and glaciers retreat. Today the general focus is on the current and projected rate of climate change, that may or may not be caused by human activities. We have seen that the climate system is a complex system with several internal and external driving forces and interconnections. Our understanding of all these processes is still not sufficient to certainly predict future climates.

We further have to consider that effects of greenhouse gases may be delayed. This implies that we cannot be sure about how much impact humans already had on the climate or how much more impact today's actions will have in the future.

:::{note} Did you know?
In 1997 the Kyoto Protocol was negotiated to reduce greenhouse gas emissions 5.2 percent below 1990 levels by 2012. The majority of the world's nations signed and ratified the treaty. The United States signed the treaty, but never became a full-fledged participant as the U.S. Senate agreed in advance not to ratify the treaty, citing economic concerns. President Bush withdrew the United States from the treaty in 2001.
:::
