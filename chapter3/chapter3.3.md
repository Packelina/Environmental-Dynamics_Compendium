(sec-3-3)=
# 3.3. Mixing

Different mixing processes between the water masses predominate depending on the characteristics of the estuary and its location. In stratified estuaries mixing occurs mainly at the halocline and at the bottom and walls of the estuary.

(sec-3-3-1)=
## 3.3.1. Entrainment

If the stratification is stable, the velocity shear across the interface between two layers of different density produces a small layer of internal waves and ripples as is shown in {ref}`fig_eddies_to_entrainment`. These small eddies of heavy saltwater are carried away, creating an upward transport of salt water and mix with the freshwater in a process called *entrainment*. Even though each wave mixing is a discrete process, averaged over time the loss of water from the lower layer to the upper can be thought of as a vertical velocity, $u_e$ .

:::{figure} ../images/fig_eddies_to_entrainment.jpg
:label: fig_eddies_to_entrainment
:width: 300px
Small wave-like eddies, responsible for entrainment, where deep water is mixed into the upper water column. @dyer1997.
:::

Entrainment is also a one-way process, where water moves from the less turbulent lower layer to a more turbulent upper layer. According to conservation of mass this one-way flux into the upper layer creates a flow magnification along the estuary and the flow rates in the top layer can increase significantly downstream, towards the mouth.

(sec-3-3-2)=
## 3.3.2. Turbulent dispersion

At less stable stratification, the mixing in the interface of two fluids with different velocities is more intense and the waves that are being created have a different form. Due to the shearing layer between the fluids a vortex sheet is formed, with pronounced eddies along the interface. Such waves are called *Kelvin-Helmholtz instabilities* and are steeper, longer and more intense than entrainment waves. They form a rolling motion between the layers and tend to break, creating turbulent dispersion across the interface and between both layers in both directions. Even though the net exchange of water between the layers is zero, the net salt flux is directed upwards, making also turbulent dispersion responsible for flow magnification in the upper layer.

(sec-3-3-3)=
## 3.3.3. Boundary layer turbulence and tidal mixing

Like in rivers and lakes, the velocity shear induced by a wall boundary layer or wind stress at the water surface creates turbulence and mixing in estuaries, both laterally and vertically. The tidal waves, flowing back and forth along the estuary also create turbulence and significant longitudinal mixing.
